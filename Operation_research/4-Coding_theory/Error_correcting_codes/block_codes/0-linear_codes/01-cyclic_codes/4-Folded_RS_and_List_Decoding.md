# Folded Reed-Solomon Codes & List Decoding

This document introduces **Folded Reed-Solomon Codes**, a variant of RS codes designed to surpass the limitations of classical unique decoding. They are famous for being the first explicit codes to achieve **List Decoding Capacity**, allowing error correction up to the information-theoretic limit ($1-R$).

---

## 1. The Limits of Classical Decoding

### 1.1 Unique Decoding
Standard decoding algorithms (like Berlekamp-Massey) are designed for **Unique Decoding**:
- **Goal**: Find the *single* codeword $c$ within distance $e$ of received word $r$.
- **Radius**: Guaranteed only if $e < d/2$.
- **Symbol Error Limit**: For RS codes ($d = n-k+1$), this means correcting up to $\approx (1-R)/2$ fraction of errors.

### 1.2 List Decoding
**List Decoding** relaxes the requirement: the decoder outputs a *list* of all codewords within radius $\rho$.
- **Goal**: Find all $c$ such that $\text{dist}(r, c) \le \rho$.
- **List Size**: Must be small (polynomially bounded in $n$).
- **The Johnson Bound**: The theoretical limit for "small lists" with standard codes is $1 - \sqrt{1-\delta}$. For RS codes, this is $\approx 1 - \sqrt{R}$.
- **Gap**: There is a significant gap between the Johnson Bound ($1-\sqrt{R}$) and the Capacity ($1-R$). Folded RS codes bridge this gap.

---

## 2. Code Folding: Definition and Construction

**Code Folding** is a technique that bundles consecutive symbols of a codeword into a larger symbol over an extension field. This increases the alphabet size but introduces algebraic dependencies between errors.

### 2.1 The $m$-Folding Process
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $[n, k, n-k+1]_q$ Reed-Solomon code generated by evaluating $f(x)$ at points $1, \gamma, \gamma^2, \dots, \gamma^{n-1}$, where $\gamma$ is a primitive element.

The **Folded Reed-Solomon Code** $FRS$ with folding parameter $m$ is defined as:
1.  **Block Length**: $N = n/m$.
2.  **Alphabet**: $\mathbb{F}_q^m$ (vectors of length $m$, or elements of $\mathbb{F}_{q^m}$).
3.  **Codewords**: The original RS codeword $(c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{n-1})$ is partitioned into blocks of size $m$:
    $$C = \left( \begin{bmatrix} c_0 \\ c_1 \\ \vdots \\ c_{m-1} \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} c_m \\ c_{m+1} \\ \vdots \\ c_{2m-1} \end{bmatrix}, \dots, \begin{bmatrix} c_{n-m} \\ \vdots \\ c_{n-1} \end{bmatrix} \right)$$

### 2.2 Algebraic View
The $j$-th symbol of the folded codeword is the vector of evaluations:
$$Y_j = \begin{bmatrix} f(\gamma^{jm}) \\ f(\gamma^{jm+1}) \\ \vdots \\ f(\gamma^{jm+m-1}) \end{bmatrix}$$
Since $\gamma^{jm+i} = \gamma^i \cdot (\gamma^m)^j$, the components are evaluations of shifts of $f(x)$.

## 3. Achieving Capacity: Guruswami-Rudra Algorithm

The structural advantage of Folded RS codes is their ability to leverage **Multivariate Interpolation** to solve the list decoding problem.

### 3.1 The Key Insight
In standard Guruswami-Sudan decoding, we interpolate a bivariate polynomial $Q(x, y)$ such that $Q(x_i, y_i) = 0$.
In Folded RS decoding, because the symbols act as a "bundle" of evaluations $(f(x), f(\gamma x), \dots, f(\gamma^{m-1}x))$, we enforce a stronger condition:
$$Q(x, f(x), f(\gamma x), \dots, f(\gamma^{m-1}x)) = 0$$
This implies $f(x)$ satisfies a specific algebraic relation that drastically reduces the number of candidate polynomials, even when the error rate is very high.

### 3.2 Main Theorem (Capacity)
> **Theorem (Guruswami-Rudra, 2006)**:
> For any rate $0 < R < 1$ and any $\epsilon > 0$, by choosing the folding parameter $m \approx O(1/\epsilon^2)$, the Folded Reed-Solomon code can be list-decoded from a fraction of errors up to:
> $$\rho = 1 - R - \epsilon$$
> with a list size of $O(1/\epsilon)$.

This result is fundamental because it proves we can correct errors right up to the information-theoretic limit (Shannon capacity for the worst-case errors) using explicit codes.

## 4. Applicability to Other Codes

### 4.1 Hamming Codes
The folding technique is **not standard** for Hamming codes.
- **Reason**: Hamming codes are "perfect" for unique decoding of single errors ($d=3$). They are high-rate ($R \to 1$) codes with very small distance.
- **Benefit**: Folding is useful for high-distance codes where the gap between $d/2$ and $d$ is large. For Hamming codes, $d=3$, so unique decoding handles 1 error. List decoding doesn't offer a meaningful improvement in radius for such small distances.

### 4.2 Parvaresh-Vardy Codes
These are precursors to Folded RS codes. Instead of folding a single polynomial $f(x)$, they transmit independent polynomials $f_1(x), \dots, f_m(x)$ that satisfy a specific algebraic constraint. Folding is often seen as a specific, more efficient instance of this general "correlated polynomial" strategy.
