# General Cyclic Codes: Algebraic Foundations

This conspectus explores the class of **Cyclic Codes**, which are linear codes with the additional property that every cyclic shift of a codeword is also a codeword. This algebraic structure allows for highly efficient encoding and decoding using polynomial rings.

## 1. Foundations: The Cyclic Shift

### 1.1 **Definition:** A linear code $\mathcal{C}$ of length $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ is **cyclic** if, for every codeword $(c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{n-1}) \in \mathcal{C}$, its cyclic shift (right shift) is also a codeword:
$$(c_{n-1}, c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{n-2}) \in \mathcal{C}$$
In operator notation, let $\sigma$ be the cyclic shift operator: $\sigma(c) \in \mathcal{C}$ for all $c \in \mathcal{C}$.

### 1.2 Polynomial Mapping
We map each vector $c \in \mathbb{F}_q^n$ to a polynomial of degree at most $n-1$ in the ring $R_n = \mathbb{F}_q[x] / (x^n - 1)$:
$$c(x) = c_0 + c_1x + c_2x^2 + \dots + c_{n-1}x^{n-1}$$
In this ring $R_n$, multiplying a polynomial by $x$ corresponds exactly to a cyclic shift:
$$x \cdot c(x) = c_0x + c_1x^2 + \dots + c_{n-1}x^n \equiv c_{n-1} + c_0x + \dots + c_{n-2}x^{n-1} \pmod{x^n - 1}$$

### 1.3 Alternative Interpretation: Matrix Representation
In linear algebra, the cyclic shift $\sigma$ can be represented by a **permutation matrix** $S$ (the companion matrix of $x^n - 1$):
$$S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & \dots & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \dots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
For any codeword $c$ (as a column vector), $Sc$ is its cyclic shift. A linear code $\mathcal{C}$ is cyclic if and only if its subspace is invariant under $S$:
$$c \in \mathcal{C} \implies Sc \in \mathcal{C}$$

**The Circulant Generator Matrix:**
Given the generator polynomial $g(x) = g_0 + g_1x + \dots + g_{n-k}x^{n-k}$, we can construct a non-systematic generator matrix $G$ using cyclic shifts of the vector $(g_0, g_1, \dots, g_{n-k}, 0, \dots, 0)$:
$$G = \begin{pmatrix} 
g_0 & g_1 & \dots & g_{n-k} & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
0 & g_0 & g_1 & \dots & g_{n-k} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \dots & g_0 & g_1 & \dots & g_{n-k}
\end{pmatrix}$$
This $k \times n$ matrix contains $k$ linearly independent rows, each being a cyclic shift of the previous one (effectively multiplication by $x$ in polynomial form).

## 2. Algebraic Structure: Codes as Ideals

**Theorem:** A linear code $\mathcal{C}$ is cyclic if and only if its polynomial representation is an **ideal** in the ring $R_n$.

**Proof Outline:**
- **Linearity** ensures closure under addition and scalar multiplication.
- **Cyclicity** (closure under $x \cdot c(x)$) combined with linearity implies closure under multiplication by any polynomial $a(x) \in R_n$:
  $$a(x)c(x) = (a_0 + a_1x + \dots)c(x) = a_0c(x) + a_1(xc(x)) + \dots \in \mathcal{C}$$
Thus, $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies the definition of an ideal.

### 2.1 Generator Polynomial $g(x)$
Since $R_n$ is a principal ideal ring, every cyclic code $\mathcal{C}$ is generated by a unique monic polynomial $g(x)$ of minimum degree.

**Properties:**
- **Divisibility:** $g(x)$ must be a divisor of $x^n - 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$.
- **Basis:** A basis for the code as a vector space is $\{g(x), xg(x), \dots, x^{k-1}g(x)\}$.
- **Dimension ($k$)**: $k = n - \deg(g(x))$. This directly sets the **Code Rate** $R = \frac{k}{n} = \frac{n - \deg(g(x))}{n}$.
- **Codewords:** Every codeword polynomial $c(x)$ can be written as $c(x) = m(x)g(x)$ for some message polynomial $m(x)$ with $\deg(m) < k$.

### 2.2 Parity-Check Polynomial $h(x)$
The parity-check polynomial $h(x)$ is defined as the "complement" of $g(x)$ in the factorization of $x^n - 1$:
$$h(x) = \frac{x^n - 1}{g(x)} = h_0 + h_1x + \dots + h_kx^k$$
A polynomial $c(x)$ is a codeword if and only if $c(x)h(x) \equiv 0 \pmod{x^n - 1}$.

### 2.3 Derivation: The Parity-Check Matrix ($H$)
To find the matrix form $Hc^T = \mathbf{0}$, we analyze the coefficients of the polynomial product $c(x)h(x) = (\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_i x^i)(\sum_{j=0}^{k} h_j x^j)$.

**Steps of Derivation:**
1.  **Identity:** Since $c(x)h(x) = m(x)(x^n - 1)$, the coefficients of the product for powers $x^k, x^{k+1}, \dots, x^{n-1}$ must be **zero**.
2.  **Coefficient formula:** The coefficient of $x^t$ in the product is given by the convolution:
    $$\text{coeff}(x^t) = \sum_{i} c_i h_{t-i}$$
3.  **Linear Equations:** Setting these to zero for $t = k, k+1, \dots, n-1$ yields $n-k$ linear equations:
    - For $t=k$: $c_0h_k + c_1h_{k-1} + \dots + c_kh_0 = 0$
    - For $t=k+1$: $c_1h_k + c_2h_{k-1} + \dots + c_{k+1}h_0 = 0$
    - $\dots$
    - For $t=n-1$: $c_{n-k-1}h_k + \dots + c_{n-1}h_0 = 0$

**Matrix Structure:**
Based on these equations, the rows of $H$ are formed by the coefficients of $h(x)$ in **reverse order** ($h_k, h_{k-1}, \dots, h_0$):
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} 
h_k & h_{k-1} & \dots & h_0 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
0 & h_k & h_{k-1} & \dots & h_0 & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \dots & h_k & h_{k-1} & \dots & h_0
\end{pmatrix}$$
This is an $(n-k) \times n$ matrix. Note that $H$ is the generator matrix for the dual code $C^\perp$.

## 3. Encoding and Syndrome Decoding

### 3.1 Systematic Encoding
To ensure the message bits appear verbatim in the codeword:
1. Shift the message: $x^{n-k}m(x)$.
2. Calculate parity: $r(x) = x^{n-k}m(x) \pmod{g(x)}$.
3. Construct codeword: $c(x) = x^{n-k}m(x) - r(x)$.

### 3.2 Syndrome Decoding: Polynomial vs. Matrix
For a received vector $v(x) = c(x) + e(x)$:

**1. Polynomial Approach (Remainder):**
The syndrome is the remainder $s(x) = v(x) \pmod{g(x)}$. This is efficient for hardware implementation using Linear Feedback Shift Registers (LFSR).

**2. Matrix Approach (Parity Checks):**
The vector syndrome $s = Hv^T$ consists of the $n-k$ parity check results.

**Derivation of Equivalence:**
The values in the vector syndrome $s = Hv^T$ are exactly the coefficients of $x^k, x^{k+1}, \dots, x^{n-1}$ in the polynomial product $v(x)h(x) \pmod{x^n - 1}$. Since $c(x)h(x) \equiv 0$, the syndrome $Hv^T$ depends purely on the error $e(x)$, mapping the algebraic remainder property into the linear algebra "null space" check.

## 4. Distance Bounds (Worst-Case Analysis)

Unlike general linear codes, the cyclic structure allows us to guarantee a minimum distance $d$ based on the **roots** of $g(x)$ in an extension field $\mathbb{F}_{q^m}$.

### 4.1 The BCH Bound
**Theorem:** Let $g(x)$ be the generator polynomial of a cyclic code of length $n$. If $g(x)$ has $\delta - 1$ consecutive powers of an $n$-th root of unity $\alpha$ as roots:
$$\alpha^b, \alpha^{b+1}, \dots, \alpha^{b+\delta-2} \text{ are roots of } g(x)$$
then the minimum distance of the code $d \geq \delta$.
The value $\delta$ is called the **designed distance**.

### 4.2 Hartmann-Tzeng (HT) Bound
The HT bound generalizes the BCH bound for sets of roots that are not strictly consecutive but follow a "shifting" sequence pattern. If $g(x)$ has roots $\alpha^{b+i_1 d_1 + i_2 d_2}$ for $0 \leq i_1 \leq \delta-2$ and $0 \leq i_2 \leq s$, then:
$$d \geq \delta + s$$
This allows for tighter distance guarantees when the generator polynomial has multiple "parallel" runs of roots.

## 5. Dual Cyclic Codes
The dual of a cyclic code $\mathcal{C}$ is also cyclic. Its generator polynomial $g^\perp(x)$ is related to the parity-check polynomial $h(x)$ by:
$$g^\perp(x) = x^k h(x^{-1})$$
(the reciprocal polynomial of $h(x)$, properly normalized).
